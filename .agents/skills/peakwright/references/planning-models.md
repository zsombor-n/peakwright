# Training Planning Models

Load this when creating full workout programs, mesocycles, sport-specific microcycles, or adjustment rules.

## Plan Types

- Performance: preserve or improve sport output. Prioritize sport practice, power, speed, conditioning specificity, strength maintenance, and fatigue management.
- Strength: prioritize squat, hinge, press, pull, bracing, and progressive intensity. Use current maxes when available; otherwise use RPE/RIR.
- Hypertrophy: prioritize weekly hard sets, stable technique, full ranges of motion, and recoverable volume.
- Endurance: prioritize aerobic base, threshold, VO2, long duration, strength maintenance, and carbohydrate availability.
- Field-court: prioritize acceleration, deceleration, change of direction, repeated sprint ability, unilateral strength, trunk stiffness, and tissue tolerance.
- Combat: prioritize strength/power, neck/trunk/hips, repeated high-intensity efforts, grip, mobility, and skill practice.
- Return-to-training: reduce novelty, avoid pain provocation, build volume before intensity, and refer out for injury management.

## Weekly Architecture

Use hard/easy sequencing:

- High neural demand: max strength, sprinting, jumps, heavy Olympic-lift derivatives, hard sparring, VO2 intervals.
- Moderate demand: hypertrophy, tempo runs, threshold, skill practice with moderate volume.
- Low demand: zone 2, technique, mobility, recovery circuits, easy aerobic work.

Default sequence for serious athletes:

1. Lower strength or acceleration
2. Aerobic/tempo/recovery or skill
3. Upper strength plus low-volume speed/COD
4. Sport conditioning or threshold
5. Total-body power or hypertrophy
6. Long aerobic, technical practice, or recovery
7. Rest

Adjust by sport calendar. Competition days replace the hardest sport-specific conditioning session.

## Session Template

Every session should include:

1. Readiness check: sleep, soreness, pain, energy, previous session response
2. Warm-up: tissue prep, mobility, activation, ramp sets or movement rehearsal
3. Power/speed first if included
4. Main strength or conditioning work
5. Assistance work matched to weak links
6. Cooldown and recovery note
7. Progression target

## Intensity Anchors

- Strength: 3-6 sets of 1-6 reps, RPE 7-9, 2-4 min rest.
- Hypertrophy: 2-5 sets of 6-15 reps, RPE 7-9, 1-3 min rest.
- Strength endurance: 2-4 sets of 12-20 reps, RPE 6-8, shorter rest.
- Power: 3-6 sets of 2-5 reps, crisp speed, stop before grinding.
- Sprint/COD: low total volume, full recovery, high quality.
- Zone 2: conversational pace, 30-90 min depending on sport and level.
- Threshold: controlled hard efforts, often 2-5 repeats of 5-12 min.
- VO2/repeated sprint: short hard intervals, enough recovery to preserve output.

## Progression Rules

- Double progression: add reps inside a range before adding load.
- RPE progression: add 2.5-5% load when all sets hit target reps at or below target RPE.
- Volume progression: add 1-2 hard sets per muscle or pattern weekly only if recovery is strong.
- Conditioning progression: increase one variable at a time: duration, reps, pace/power, or reduced rest.
- Deload: reduce volume 30-50%, maintain light technique, and keep intensity moderate unless the athlete is exhausted.

## Monitoring

Track at least:

- Bodyweight trend: 3-7 day average
- Training log: load, reps, distance, pace, RPE
- Sleep: duration and quality
- Soreness/pain: 0-10
- Appetite and mood
- Sport output: sprint time, jump, bar speed, pace/power, skill quality

Adjustment rules:

- If performance improves and soreness is normal: progress one variable.
- If performance stalls for two similar sessions: hold load and reduce accessories by 10-20%.
- If pain changes technique: stop the provoking movement and substitute pain-free range/pattern.
- If sleep is poor for 2-3 nights: reduce high-intensity volume.
- If bodyweight change misses the goal for two weeks: adjust calories by 150-250 kcal/day.
